Turkish Grammar Guide

Turkish builds words like Lego: a root plus a chain of suffixes, each with one job. Learn the handful of rules below and thousands of word forms become predictable. Sections are marked Beginner Intermediate Advanced so you can pick your level.

Contents

    Beginner

    Alphabet and pronunciation

    Turkish uses 29 Latin letters and is written exactly as it sounds: every letter is always pronounced the same way. There are no silent letters.

    Most letters sound roughly like English. These are the ones to learn:

    LetterSounds likeExample
    cj in jamcevap — answer
    çch in chairçay — tea
    şsh in shopşimdi — now
    ğsilent; lengthens the vowel before itdağ (daa) — mountain
    ıundotted i: like the e in open, unroundedkız — girl
    iee in see (short)iki — two
    öGerman ö: say e with rounded lipsgöz — eye
    üGerman ü: say ee with rounded lipsüç — three
    js in measuregaraj — garage
    rlightly tappedbir — one
    Watch out: ı/I and i/İ are different letters, and each has its own capital: ı → I, i → İ. So İstanbul starts with a dotted capital.

    Stress usually falls on the last syllable: ki-TAP, kitap-LAR. Place names and some suffixes (like the negative -me) are exceptions: AN-ka-ra, GEL-me (don't come).

    Beginner

    Vowel harmony

    This is the most important rule in Turkish. Suffixes change their vowel to match the last vowel of the word they attach to. Vowels come in two groups:

    UnroundedRounded
    Fronte, iö, ü
    Backa, ıo, u

    2-way harmony: e / a

    Suffixes like the plural -ler/-lar have two forms. Use e after a front vowel and a after a back vowel.

    Last vowelSuffix vowelExample
    e, i, ö, üeevler houses, gözler eyes
    a, ı, o, uakızlar girls, okullar schools

    4-way harmony: i / ı / u / ü

    Suffixes with a high vowel, like the question particle mi, have four forms:

    Last vowelSuffix vowelExample
    e, iievim my house
    a, ııkızım my daughter
    o, uuokulum my school
    ö, üügözüm my eye
    Notation: grammar books write suffixes with capital letters for the changing parts: -lAr means -ler/-lar, and -(I)m means -im/-ım/-um/-üm. This guide lists the forms instead.

    This is why the quiz has misin, mısın, musun and müsün: they're the same word ("are you?") in harmony with different verbs.

    Beginner

    Consonant changes

    Hard consonants make d → t (and c → ç)

    The consonants ç, f, h, k, p, s, ş, t are "hard". Turkish students remember them with the phrase "FıSTıKÇı ŞaHaP". After them, a suffix starting with d becomes t, and one starting with c becomes ç:

    evde at home → kitapta in the book · geldi came → gitti went · sütçü milkman

    Softening: p, ç, t, k → b, c, d, ğ

    When a vowel-initial suffix is added, a final p, ç, t, k in most words of more than one syllable softens:

    Word+ vowel suffix
    kitap (book)kitabı — the book / his book
    ağaç (tree)ağacı
    çocuk (child)çocuğu — his child (in the quiz!)
    renk (colour)rengi — after n, k becomes g
    git- (go), et- (do)gidiyorum, ederim

    Most one-syllable words don't soften (top → topu, at → atı), and neither do many loanwords (saat → saati).

    Buffer letters: y, n, s

    Turkish avoids two vowels side by side. When a vowel suffix meets a word ending in a vowel, a buffer consonant slips in:

    • y for most suffixes: arabaya to the car, neyi what (object)
    • s for the "his/her/its" suffix: arabası his car
    • n for the genitive and after "his/her/its": arabanın of the car, evinde in his house
    Beginner

    How words are built

    Turkish is agglutinative: instead of separate words like "my", "in", "the" or "were", it adds suffixes to the end of a root. The order is fixed:

    root → (derivation) → plural → possessive → case → (-ki) → "to be" / person
    WordMeaning
    evhouse
    evlerhouses
    evlerimmy houses
    evlerimdein my houses
    evlerimdekithe one in my houses
    evlerimdekilerthe ones in my houses

    Verbs work the same way: gelmeyecekler = come + not + will + they = "they will not come".

    Beginner

    Word order, articles and gender

    • Verb last. The basic order is Subject – Object – Verb: Ben çay içiyorum = "I tea am-drinking".
    • No gender. o means he, she and it.
    • No "the". Definiteness is shown by the object ending (see cases). bir ("one") works as "a/an": bir kitap = a book.
    • Pronouns are often dropped, because the verb ending already shows the person: Geliyorum = I'm coming.
    • Adjectives come before nouns and never change: güzel ev, güzel evler.
    • Modifiers come before what they modify, so English "after the film" becomes filmden sonra, "the film-from after".
    Beginner

    Plural: -ler / -lar

    Add -ler or -lar following 2-way harmony: kitaplar books, şeyler things, çocuklar children.

    Remember: after a number or a word like çok (many) or birkaç (a few), the noun stays singular: iki kitap (two books), çok insan (many people).

    Beginner

    "To be", var and yok

    Turkish has no separate word for "am/is/are". You add a personal ending to the noun or adjective:

    PersonEndingöğretmen (teacher)mutlu (happy)Negative
    I am-(y)im/ım/um/ümöğretmenimmutluyumdeğilim
    you are-sin/sın/sun/sünöğretmensinmutlusundeğilsin
    he/she is— (-dir)öğretmenmutludeğil
    we are-(y)iz/ız/uz/üzöğretmenizmutluyuzdeğiliz
    you are (pl.)-siniz/sınız/sunuz/sünüzöğretmensinizmutlusunuzdeğilsiniz
    they are-(ler)öğretmenlermutlulardeğiller

    -dir/-dır/-dur/-dür adds "is" in a factual or certain tone: Önemlidir "It is important". It's common in writing, and in speech it often means "probably": Evdedir "He's probably at home".

    Past "was": add -(y)dı + person: mutluydum I was happy, evdeydi he was at home, değildi wasn't.

    var / yok: there is / there isn't, have / don't have

    Burada bir sorun var.There is a problem here.
    Zaman yok.There's no time.

    To say "I have", say "my ___ exists": Param var = I have money (lit. "my-money exists"). Arabam yok = I don't have a car.

    Beginner

    The six cases

    Where English uses prepositions (to, at, from), Turkish uses case endings. Each one follows vowel harmony and the consonant rules above.

    CaseEndingMeaningExamples
    Nominative—subject, indefinite objectev, okul
    Accusative-(y)i/ı/u/üthe (definite object)evi, arabayı
    Dative-(y)e/ato, towardseve home, okula to school
    Locative-de/da/te/tain, at, onevde at home, kitapta
    Ablative-den/dan/ten/tanfrom, out of, thanevden, Türkiye'den
    Genitive-(n)in/ın/un/ünof, 'sevin, arabanın

    Also common: with = ile or the suffix -(y)le/la: arabayla by car, arkadaşımla with my friend.

    Definite vs. indefinite object

    Kitap okuyorum.I'm reading (a book / books) — no particular book.
    Kitabı okuyorum.I'm reading the book — a specific one.

    Proper nouns take an apostrophe

    İstanbul'da in Istanbul, Ali'ye to Ali, Ayşe'nin Ayşe's.

    Possession: "the man's car"

    Both sides get marked: genitive on the owner, possessive on the thing owned.

    Ali'nin arabasıAli's car
    evin kapısıthe door of the house

    Without the genitive, it becomes a compound noun: Türk kahvesi Turkish coffee, doğum günü birthday.

    Questions

    Yes/no questions: mi / mı / mu / mü

    Put the particle mi (written separately, but following 4-way harmony) after the word you're asking about:

    Çay mı istiyorsun?Is it tea you want?
    Çay istiyor musun?Do you want tea?
    Öğretmen misin?Are you a teacher?
    Geldin mi?Did you come? (past tense: the person ending stays on the verb)

    Question words

    TurkishEnglishTurkishEnglish
    newhatneredewhere (at)
    kimwhonereyewhere (to)
    neden / niye / niçinwhyneredenwhere from

    Question words take endings too: kimi whom, kime to whom, kimin whose, neyi what (object), nasılsın how are you.

    Don't use mi together with a question word: Neredesin? "Where are you?" needs nothing else.

    Beginner

    Verbs and the present tense (-iyor)

    The dictionary form ends in -mek/-mak. Remove it to get the stem: gelmek → gel- (come), yapmak → yap- (do).

    The present continuous (-iyor) covers both "I am doing" and, in speech, "I do". Structure: stem + iyor + person.

    PersonEndinggelmekyapmakNegative
    I-umgeliyorumyapıyorumgelmiyorum
    you-sungeliyorsunyapıyorsungelmiyorsun
    he/she—geliyoryapıyorgelmiyor
    we-uzgeliyoruzyapıyoruzgelmiyoruz
    you (pl.)-sunuzgeliyorsunuzyapıyorsunuzgelmiyorsunuz
    they-largeliyorlaryapıyorlargelmiyorlar
    • The first vowel follows 4-way harmony: geliyor, yapıyor, okuyor → okuyor, görüyor. The -yor part never changes, so the endings after it are always -um, -sun, -uz…
    • A stem ending in e/a loses that vowel: iste- → istiyorum (I want), bekle- → bekliyor, anla- → anlıyorum.
    • Negative: add -me/-ma before -iyor. Its vowel then changes: bil-me-iyor → bilmiyorum (I don't know).
    • Question: Geliyor musun? Are you coming?

    Intermediate

    Simple present / aorist (-r)

    The aorist expresses habits, general truths, willingness and polite offers: Kahve içer misin? Would you drink some coffee?

    • Stem ends in a vowel → -r: okur, bekler, söyler
    • One-syllable stem → usually -er/-ar: yapar, git → gider, içer
    • Multi-syllable stem → -ir/ır/ur/ür: konuşur, çalışır
    • 13 one-syllable exceptions take -ir: al, bil, bul, dur, gel, gör, kal, ol, öl, san, var, ver, vur → gelir, olur, bilir, verir…

    The negative is irregular:

    PersonPositiveNegative
    Iyaparımyapmam
    youyaparsınyapmazsın
    he/sheyaparyapmaz
    weyaparızyapmayız
    you (pl.)yaparsınızyapmazsınız
    theyyaparlaryapmazlar

    Common fixed phrases: Teşekkür ederim (thank you, lit. "I do thanks"), Görüşürüz (see you), Olur (OK, it'll do), Olmaz (no way), Bilmem (dunno).

    Intermediate

    Reported past (-miş)

    -miş/-mış/-muş/-müş is for things you didn't witness: hearsay, inference, or surprise. English often adds "apparently" or "I heard".

    Ali geldi.Ali came. (I saw it)
    Ali gelmiş.Apparently Ali came. / Ali has come (I see his shoes by the door).
    Çok güzelmiş!Oh, it's really nice! (discovering it now)

    Endings: gelmişim, gelmişsin, gelmiş, gelmişiz, gelmişsiniz, gelmişler. Negative: gelmemiş. Fairy tales start with Bir varmış, bir yokmuş… "Once there was, once there wasn't…"

    Location nouns

    Place words are nouns that take possessive + case: masanın üstünde on the table, evin önünde in front of the house. Others: alt under, iç inside, yan beside, arka behind, ara between. That's where quiz words like içinde, altında, yanında, arasında come from.

    Advanced

    Participles: relative clauses

    Turkish has no word for "who/which/that". It turns the verb into an adjective that goes before the noun.

    SuffixWhen to useExample
    -(y)en/-(y)anthe noun is the subjectgelen adam the man who comes/came
    olan the one that is / what happened
    -dik/dık/duk/dük + poss.the noun is the object/other, past or presentokuduğum kitap the book I read / am reading
    istediğin what you want
    -(y)ecek/acak + poss.object/other, futureokuyacağım kitap the book I will read
    Dün gördüğüm film çok güzeldi.The film (that) I saw yesterday was very good.
    Türkçe bilen biri var mı?Is there someone who knows Turkish?

    Many quiz words are participles: yaptığın what you did, istediğim what I want, gelen incoming, kalan remaining.

    Verbal nouns: "that…" clauses

    English "that you came" or "to go" becomes a noun built from the verb, which then takes case endings like any noun.

    SuffixUseExample
    -mek/-makinfinitive, "to do"Yüzmek istiyorum. I want to swim.
    -me/-ma + poss.action, wishes and ordersGelmeni istiyorum. I want you to come.
    -dik + poss.facts, "that…"Geldiğini biliyorum. I know that you came.
    Haklı olduğunu that he is right
    -(y)eceğ + poss.future factsGeleceğini söyledi. He said he would come.
    -iş/-ış…manner, the act ofgidiş going/departure, bakış a look, giriş entrance

    This is why olduğunu is one of the most common words in the quiz: olduğunu = be + fact + his + object, "that he/it is".

    Advanced

    Voice: passive, causative, reflexive, reciprocal

    Suffixes right after the stem change who does what. They come before the negative and the tense.

    VoiceSuffixExample
    Passive-il / -in / -nyap → yapıldı it was done, oku → okunur is read, bil → bilinen known
    Causative-dir / -t / -ir / -itöl → öldürmek to kill (make die), bekle → bekletmek to keep waiting, yap → yaptırmak to have something done
    Reflexive-in / -nyıka → yıkanmak to wash oneself, giy → giyinmek to get dressed
    Reciprocal-iş / -şgör → görüşmek to see each other, tanı → tanışmak to meet

    These stack, too: öldürüldü he was killed (die + cause + passive + past). And görüşürüz "see you" is literally "we will see each other".

    Advanced

    Expressive features

    Intensive adjectives

    Repeat the first syllable, closed with m, p, r or s, to mean "completely": beyaz → bembeyaz snow-white, kara → kapkara pitch-black, mavi → masmavi, yeşil → yemyeşil, temiz → tertemiz spotless.

    m-reduplication: "and stuff"

    Repeat a word with m replacing its first sound for a vague "and whatnot": kitap mitap books and such, çay may tea and stuff.

    Doubled words

    yavaş yavaş slowly, gradually · ara sıra now and then · güle güle bye (said by the one staying, lit. "laughing laughing").

    The -ki suffix

    -ki turns a location or time into an adjective or pronoun: masadaki kitap the book on the table, bugünkü gazete today's paper, benimki mine.

    The record word

    Showing off agglutination:

    Avrupalılaştıramadıklarımızdanmışsınız"You are apparently one of those whom we could not make European."

    Europe · from · become · cause · cannot · (participle) · plural · our · from · apparently · you are.
